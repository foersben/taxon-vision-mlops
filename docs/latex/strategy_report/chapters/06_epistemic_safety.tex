\chapter{Epistemic Safety \& Active Learning}
\label{ch:safety}

Deploying deep neural networks within automated ecological monitoring workflows introduces a fundamental operational risk: discriminative classifiers optimised under cross-entropy objectives are systematically uncalibrated and prone to overconfident misclassifications. Because the softmax operator projects arbitrary logit vectors onto the probability simplex, the resulting class assignment is invariant to constant scalar shifts and sensitive only to relative logit differences. When confronted with motion-blurred field imagery, non-biological sensor artefacts, or uncatalogued taxa from outside the training distribution, standard vision architectures frequently concentrate probability mass onto a single erroneous category. In biodiversity informatics, such silent misclassifications corrupt longitudinal occurrence registries and distort downstream conservation models.

To establish verifiable production reliability, the TaxonVision-MLOps serving architecture decouples representation extraction from raw point predictions. The inference pipeline integrates three sequential mechanisms into a unified epistemic safety layer: pre-softmax Helmholtz free-energy filtering, deterministic spatial-temporal logit masking, and distribution-free Split Conformal Prediction. Together, these stages enforce bounded marginal error rates under arbitrary data distributions and programmatically route statistically ambiguous or novel observations into a human-in-the-loop active learning cycle.

\section{Energy-Based Out-of-Distribution Detection}
\label{sec:energy_ood}

Unconstrained citizen-science data streams continuously introduce non-biological clutter, corrupted image payloads, and organisms belonging to unmodelled phyla. Constructing prediction sets or executing vector retrieval over such inputs wastes computational cycles and contaminates operational telemetry. Consequently, the initial gate of the safety layer intercepts and rejects out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs directly within the unnormalised logit space prior to probability normalisation.

Conventional anomaly detectors rely on the Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP), defined as $\max_j \hat{\pi}_j(x)$. However, because the unit-sum constraint $\sum_{j=1}^K \hat{\pi}_j(x) = 1$ forces probability mass to be conserved regardless of absolute activation magnitude, MSP exhibits high false-positive rates on out-of-distribution samples that trigger weak, near-uniform feature responses with a slight random perturbation. To circumvent this normalisation pathology, the platform evaluates the scalar Helmholtz free energy of the logit vector. Borrowed from statistical mechanics, the free energy $E(x; f)$ quantifies the negative log-partition function of the discriminative model, acting as a surrogate for the negative log-density of the input $x \in \mathcal{X}$ under the learned data manifold.

Let $f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^K$ denote the parameterised neural mapping from the normalised visual crop $x$ to the unnormalised logit vector $f(x) = (f_1(x), \dots, f_K(x))^\top$ across $K$ operational taxa. Under the Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution, the categorical likelihood of class $j \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ at temperature $T \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is coupled to the scalar free energy function $E(x; f)$ via:
\begin{equation}
E(x; f) = -T \cdot \log \sum_{j=1}^{K} \exp\left(\frac{f_j(x)}{T}\right)
\label{eq:helmholtz_energy}
\end{equation}
where $T > 0$ denotes the temperature scaling parameter governing the smoothness of the log-sum-exp aggregation (fixed to $T = 1.0$ during standard inference). Inputs aligned with the learned training manifold elicit strong positive activations on compatible taxonomic classes, driving the partition sum upwards and yielding low (strongly negative) free energy values. Conversely, out-of-distribution anomalies fail to excite class-specific feature detectors, resulting in suppressed logit magnitudes and elevated free energy states.

\subsection{Orthogonal Epistemic Defense via Predictive Shannon Entropy}
\label{subsec:predictive_entropy}

Whilst the scalar Helmholtz free energy effectively detects non-biological noise, background clutter, and sensor dropouts that fail to elicit feature activations, energy thresholding alone exhibits an intrinsic vulnerability under fine-grained biological domain shifts. When confronted with an out-of-perimeter organism from an unmodelled biological class or phylum -- for example, an insect larva or caterpillar submitted to a model trained exclusively on botanical taxa -- the input features may align weakly with multiple disparate class weights simultaneously. The cumulative inner products across several classes can artificially inflate the partition sum $\sum_{j=1}^K \exp(f_j(x)/T)$, driving the free energy below $\tau_{\text{OOD}}$ despite the absence of any true botanical morphological homology.

To prevent such out-of-perimeter specimens from silently clearing the OOD gate, the platform couples Helmholtz energy with the predictive Shannon entropy $H(\hat{\pi}(x))$ of the softmax distribution. For the probability vector $\hat{\pi}(x) = (\hat{\pi}_1(x), \dots, \hat{\pi}_K(x))^\top \in \Delta^{K-1}$, the predictive entropy is evaluated in nats:
\begin{equation}
H(\hat{\pi}(x)) = -\sum_{j=1}^{K} \hat{\pi}_j(x) \log \left( \hat{\pi}_j(x) + \epsilon \right)
\label{eq:shannon_entropy}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$ provides numerical stability. The predictive entropy quantifies categorical dispersion: confident, decisive assignments yield $H \to 0$, whereas severe epistemic indecision over ambiguous or out-of-perimeter taxa drives $H$ towards the uniform maximum $\log(K)$ ($H_{\max} = \log(10) \approx 2.3026\text{ nats}$ for $K = 10$).

During real-time serving, the system evaluates both safety boundaries simultaneously. Downstream execution is governed by the composite binary gating operator $\Phi(x)$:
\begin{equation}
\Phi(x) = 
\begin{cases}
    1 \quad (\text{In-Distribution: Admissible}), & \text{if } E(x; f) \le \tau_{\text{OOD}} \ \wedge \ H(\hat{\pi}(x)) \le \tau_{\text{entropy}}, \\
    0 \quad (\text{Out-of-Distribution: Triage}), & \text{if } E(x; f) > \tau_{\text{OOD}} \ \vee \ H(\hat{\pi}(x)) > \tau_{\text{entropy}}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:dual_ood_gate}
\end{equation}
where the entropy threshold is calibrated to $\tau_{\text{entropy}} = 1.60\text{ nats}$ (persisted in \nolinkurl{config/default_config.yaml}). This dual-gate formulation guarantees that an input is admitted to spatial-temporal masking if and only if it exhibits both sufficient overall feature activation mass ($E \le \tau_{\text{OOD}}$) and decisive categorical concentration ($H \le \tau_{\text{entropy}}$).

\subsection{Analytical Bounds, Threshold Calibration, and Recalibration Dynamics}
\label{subsec:energy_calibration_math}

Unlike normalised probabilities, which are strictly confined to the unit interval $[0, 1]$, Helmholtz free energy operates on an unconstrained scale whose domain depends directly on the taxonomic cardinality $K$, the temperature $T$, and the $L_2$ weight-decay regularisation $\lambda$ of the classification head. Adopting arbitrary static thresholds from external literature -- such as $\tau_{\text{OOD}} = -12.5$, derived from unregularised $1{,}000$-class ImageNet benchmarks -- violates the mathematical domain of compact linear heads and induces complete rejection of valid observations. Establishing a rigorous decision boundary therefore requires deriving the analytical upper and lower energy asymptotes of the classifier.

\paragraph{Upper Energy Bound (Maximum Entropy and the Noise Floor).}
Consider an uninformative input $x_{\text{noise}}$ (such as background foliage, sensor blur, or an unmodelled phylum) that shares zero morphological features with the $K$ catalogued taxa. Because the frozen vision backbone embeddings are unit-normalised ($\|z\|_2 = 1$) and the linear head weights are regularised towards the origin, the inner products vanish and the logit vector collapses towards zero-centred uniform indifference, $f_j(x_{\text{noise}}) \to 0$ for all $j \in \{1, \dots, K\}$. Substituting this zero-activation state into Equation~\ref{eq:helmholtz_energy} yields the analytical noise ceiling $E_{\text{noise}}$:
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{noise}}(K, T) = -T \cdot \log \left( \sum_{j=1}^{K} \exp(0) \right) = -T \cdot \log(K)
\label{eq:energy_upper_bound}
\end{equation}
For the baseline operational catalogue of $K = 10$ taxa at unit temperature ($T = 1.0$), this upper bound evaluates to $E_{\text{noise}} = -\log(10) \approx -2.3026$. Physically, $E_{\text{noise}}$ represents the state of maximum thermodynamic entropy: any observation yielding $E(x; f) \ge -2.3026$ possesses less total activation mass than uniform random noise and is deterministically classified as out-of-distribution clutter.

\paragraph{Lower Energy Bound (In-Distribution Activation Regime).}
To determine the lower energy floor $E_{\min}$ attainable by genuine biological specimens, let $f_{\max}(x) = \max_j f_j(x)$ denote the dominant class logit, and let $\hat{\pi}_{\max}(x) = \exp(f_{\max}(x)/T) / \sum_j \exp(f_j(x)/T)$ denote the corresponding maximum softmax probability. Factoring $\exp(f_{\max}(x)/T)$ out of the partition sum in Equation~\ref{eq:helmholtz_energy} yields the exact algebraic identity:
\begin{equation}
E(x; f) = -f_{\max}(x) + T \cdot \log \hat{\pi}_{\max}(x)
\label{eq:energy_identity}
\end{equation}
Because $\hat{\pi}_{\max}(x) \in [1/K, 1]$, the logarithmic correction term is strictly bounded within $\log \hat{\pi}_{\max}(x) \in [-\log K, 0]$. For a high-confidence in-distribution specimen where $\hat{\pi}_{\max}(x) \to 1$, the second term vanishes ($\log \hat{\pi}_{\max}(x) \to 0$), and the free energy converges asymptotically to the negative dominant logit, $E(x; f) \approx -f_{\max}(x)$. Under an $L_2$-regularised linear classification head with bounded weight norm $\|W\|_\infty$ operating on unit-hypersphere embeddings, the maximum achievable logit is bounded by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, $f_{\max}(x) \le \|w_{\max}\|_2 \|z\|_2 + b_{\max}$. Empirically, across well-converged DINOv3 and BioCLIP-2 heads, prototypical specimens generate dominant logits in the interval $f_{\max}(x) \in [3.5, 4.8]$ with $\hat{\pi}_{\max}(x) \ge 0.85$, confining legitimate in-distribution observations to the energy band $E(x; f) \in [-4.80, -3.50]$. By contrast, an uncalibrated threshold of $\tau = -12.5$ would demand $f_{\max}(x) \ge 12.5$ ($\exp(f_{\max}) \approx 2.68 \times 10^5$), a regime mathematically unreachable under weight-decay regularisation.

\paragraph{Practical Threshold Calibration and Continuous Recalibration.}
Rather than relying on manual heuristics, $\tau_{\text{OOD}}$ is determined empirically as an automated step at the conclusion of classification head training. Given the holdout in-distribution validation split $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{m}$, the calibration module (\texttt{EnergyOODDetector.calibrate\_threshold\_from\_logits}) computes the empirical energy distribution $\mathcal{E}_{\text{val}} = \{E(x_i; f)\}_{i=1}^{m}$ and sets $\tau_{\text{OOD}}$ to the $(1 - \beta)$-quantile corresponding to a prescribed True Positive Rate (TPR) of $1 - \beta = 0.95$:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\text{OOD}} = \text{Quantile}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\text{val}}; \, 1 - \beta\right) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \tau_{\text{OOD}} < -T \cdot \log(K)
\label{eq:ood_calibration}
\end{equation}
For the $K = 10$ baseline configuration, this protocol yields $\tau_{\text{OOD}} = -2.40$ (persisted in \nolinkurl{config/default_config.yaml}), providing a conservative safety margin below the $-2.3026$ noise floor whilst retaining $95\%$ of valid holdout specimens. During pipeline execution, the exact calibrated threshold boundary is logged directly into MLflow and annotated onto the validation energy histogram artifact (\nolinkurl{visualizations/energy_distribution.png}).

Because the energy scale is intrinsically coupled to the logit geometry of the active model, $\tau_{\text{OOD}}$ is not a static constant and must undergo automated recalibration whenever one of three structural transitions occurs:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Taxonomic Cardinality Expansion ($K \to K'$):} The baseline noise ceiling shifts logarithmically according to $\Delta E_{\text{noise}} = -T \cdot \log(K' / K)$. Scaling the catalogue from $K = 10$ to $K' = 2{,}000$ taxa shifts the uniform noise floor from $-2.30$ to $-\log(2000) \approx -7.60$, requiring an immediate recomputation of Equation~\ref{eq:ood_calibration}.
    \item \textbf{Head Retraining and Hyperparameter Updates:} Modifications to the Optuna-tuned weight-decay penalty $\lambda$, dropout rate $p$, or Class-Balanced Loss factor $\beta_{\text{CB}}$ alter the $L_2$ norm of the linear head weights $\|W\|_2$, rescaling $f_{\max}(x)$ and shifting the empirical energy distribution $\mathcal{E}_{\text{val}}$.
    \item \textbf{Covariate Shift and Drift-Triggered Recalibration:} When the real-time Prometheus drift monitor detects a statistically significant shift in unit-embedding norms ($W_1(\mathcal{Z}_{\text{base}}, \mathcal{Z}_{\text{prod}}) > \tau_{\text{drift}}$) or an anomalous spike in the OOD rejection rate, the CI/CD pipeline triggers a recalibration pass over the updated DVC validation manifest prior to promoting the next \texttt{Challenger} model.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Spatial-Temporal Constraint Masking}
\label{sec:spatial_masking}

Visual classification in high-dimensional representation spaces is vulnerable to convergent evolution, wherein taxonomically distinct species inhabiting separate biogeographical realms evolve near-identical morphological traits. Whilst a purely visual classifier assigns comparable activation scores to such allopatric sibling taxa, physical geography and seasonal phenology impose strict deterministic constraints.

To enforce biogeographical validity without introducing secondary neural network latency, the serving engine applies a deterministic boolean mask directly to the post-OOD logit vector. Incoming citizen-science observations carry DarwinCore spatial-temporal metadata comprising WGS84 coordinates and an acquisition timestamp. During ingestion, the coordinates are projected onto a discrete Uber H3 hexagonal cell index $h \in \mathcal{H}$ (Resolution 4), and the timestamp is mapped to a calendar month $m \in \{1, \dots, 12\}$.

Let $\mathcal{A}(h, m) \subseteq \{1, \dots, K\}$ denote the admissible taxon subset derived from verified historical GBIF occurrence records within a geodesic radius $R_{\max}$ of cell $h$ during the seasonal window $(m \pm 1) \pmod{12}$. The spatially constrained logit vector $\tilde{f}(x) \in (\mathbb{R} \cup \{-\infty\})^K$ is obtained element-wise for each taxon $j \in \{1, \dots, K\}$:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{f}_j(x) = 
\begin{cases}
    f_j(x), & \text{if } j \in \mathcal{A}(h, m) \\
    -\infty, & \text{if } j \notin \mathcal{A}(h, m)
\end{cases}
\label{eq:spatial_mask}
\end{equation}
Projecting $\tilde{f}(x)$ onto the probability simplex via the softmax operator yields the biogeographically constrained categorical distribution $\hat{\pi}(x) \in \Delta^{K-1}$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\pi}_j(x) = \frac{\exp(\tilde{f}_j(x))}{\sum_{k=1}^K \exp(\tilde{f}_k(x))}
\label{eq:constrained_softmax}
\end{equation}
Because $\exp(-\infty) = 0$, any taxon absent from the admissible regional and seasonal niche $\mathcal{A}(h, m)$ is assigned strictly zero probability mass ($\hat{\pi}_j(x) = 0$), redistributing the normalised density exclusively across ecologically plausible candidates prior to uncertainty quantification.

\section{Distribution-Free Uncertainty via Split Conformal Prediction}
\label{sec:conformal_prediction}

Even after out-of-distribution filtering and biogeographical masking, point predictions derived from $\arg\max_j \hat{\pi}_j(x)$ lack formal statistical validity. To bound the operational error rate without relying on distributional assumptions or asymptotic approximations, the serving architecture wraps the constrained probability estimator in a Split Conformal Prediction layer \cite{angelopoulos2021gentle}. This transformation converts raw point estimates into dynamic prediction sets $\hat{C}(X) \subseteq \{1, \dots, K\}$ accompanied by a rigorous, finite-sample marginal coverage guarantee.

\subsection{Calibration Protocol and Finite-Sample Quantile Estimation}
\label{subsec:conformal_calibration}

Let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ denote an independent calibration split comprising $n$ exchangeable observation pairs, held strictly disjoint from the training and hyperparameter validation partitions. For each calibration instance $(x_i, y_i)$ with ground-truth Linnaean class index $y_i \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, the system evaluates a scalar non-conformity score $s_i \in [0, 1]$ defined as the complement of the constrained softmax probability assigned to the true taxon:
\begin{equation}
s_i = s(x_i, y_i) = 1 - \hat{\pi}_{y_i}(x_i)
\label{eq:nonconformity_score}
\end{equation}
The score $s_i$ quantifies the degree of epistemic disagreement between the model prediction and the ground truth: confident, correct classifications yield $s_i \to 0$, whereas severely miscalibrated or ambiguous predictions yield $s_i \to 1$.

For a user-prescribed miscoverage significance level $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ (fixed at $\alpha = 0.05$ to enforce a $95\%$ nominal coverage target), the calibration engine sorts the non-conformity scores in non-decreasing order, $s_{(1)} \le s_{(2)} \le \dots \le s_{(n)}$, and computes the finite-sample corrected empirical quantile $\hat{q}_\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{q}_\alpha = \text{Quantile}\left( \{s_1, \dots, s_n\}; \, \frac{\lceil (n + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil}{n} \right) = s_{(\lceil (n + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil)}
\label{eq:conformal_quantile}
\end{equation}
where the ceiling correction $\lceil (n + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil / n$ accounts for the discrete rank granularity of the finite sample size $n$, preventing systematic under-coverage on unseen test distributions.

\subsection{Prediction Set Construction and Marginal Coverage Guarantee}
\label{subsec:conformal_guarantee}

During live inference on a newly submitted field observation $X_{n+1}$ with unobserved true label $Y_{n+1} \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, the conformal gate constructs the prediction set $\hat{C}(X_{n+1})$ by admitting all candidate taxa whose non-conformity scores do not exceed the calibrated cutoff $\hat{q}_\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{C}(X_{n+1}) = \left\{ y \in \{1, \dots, K\} : 1 - \hat{\pi}_y(X_{n+1}) \le \hat{q}_\alpha \right\} = \left\{ y \in \{1, \dots, K\} : \hat{\pi}_y(X_{n+1}) \ge 1 - \hat{q}_\alpha \right\}
\label{eq:conformal_set}
\end{equation}
Under the sole assumption that the calibration tuples $\{(X_i, Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ and the test tuple $(X_{n+1}, Y_{n+1})$ are exchangeable, the ranks of the non-conformity scores $\{s_1, \dots, s_{n+1}\}$ are uniformly distributed over the permutations of $\{1, \dots, n+1\}$. Consequently, the constructed set $\hat{C}(X_{n+1})$ satisfies the finite-sample marginal coverage theorem:
\begin{equation}
1 - \alpha \le \mathbb{P}\left(Y_{n+1} \in \hat{C}(X_{n+1})\right) \le 1 - \alpha + \frac{1}{n + 1}
\label{eq:coverage_theorem}
\end{equation}
Crucially, Equation~\ref{eq:coverage_theorem} holds across arbitrary visual feature distributions and base classifier architectures. The cardinality $|\hat{C}(X_{n+1})|$ adapts natively to local input difficulty: unambiguous, high-signal photographs collapse to singleton sets ($|\hat{C}| = 1$), whilst partially occluded or morphologically cryptic specimens expand into multi-element sets that faithfully bound the model's epistemic uncertainty.

\section{Conformal Triage Routing \& Active Learning Feedback}
\label{sec:active_learning_triage}

The prediction set cardinality $|\hat{C}(X_{n+1})|$ coupled with the maximum softmax probability $\hat{\pi}_{\max}(X_{n+1}) = \max_{y} \hat{\pi}_y(X_{n+1})$ serves as a deterministic control signal at the FastAPI serving boundary, partitioning incoming inference traffic into a four-tier operational execution contract:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decisive Fast-Path Resolution ($|\hat{C}(X_{n+1})| = 1$ with $\hat{\pi}_{\max} \ge \tau_{\text{conf}}$):} Exactly one taxon satisfies $\hat{\pi}_y(X_{n+1}) \ge 1 - \hat{q}_\alpha$, and the top-1 confidence simultaneously satisfies the operational confidence threshold $\hat{\pi}_{\max}(X_{n+1}) \ge \tau_{\text{conf}}$ (calibrated to $\tau_{\text{conf}} = 0.60$ in \nolinkurl{config/conformal_config.yaml}). The classification is statistically conclusive at the $1 - \alpha$ significance level and is returned immediately by the INT8 ONNX runtime within the sub-$25\text{ ms}$ hot-path SLA, completely bypassing secondary retrieval and operator triage.
    \item \textbf{Moderate Ambiguity ($1 < |\hat{C}(X_{n+1})| \le k_{\max}$, with $k_{\max} = 3$):} The conformal prediction set contains between two and three closely related sibling taxa. The serving engine lazy-loads Pipeline~2's LanceDB index, executes a single-stage filtered HNSW traversal constrained to the candidate classes and spatio-temporal metadata, and performs Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) over exemplar crops with under $6\text{ ms}$ overhead to deliver a multi-candidate comparative differential to the operator.
    \item \textbf{Severe Ambiguity or Low Top-1 Confidence ($|\hat{C}(X_{n+1})| > k_{\max}$ or $\hat{\pi}_{\max} < \tau_{\text{conf}}$):} Automated assignment is halted when the set cardinality exceeds the ambiguity ceiling $k_{\max}$ or when a singleton set fails the minimum confidence threshold ($\hat{\pi}_{\max} < 0.60$). The latter condition accounts for diffuse predictive states where a single class dominates only marginally (for example, $\hat{\pi}_{\max} = 0.482$ with $1 - \hat{q}_\alpha = 0.15$), which would otherwise evade set-size gating. The observation is scheduled for asynchronous multimodal resolution via gRPC to the quantised visual language model (\texttt{taxon-vision-vlm}, based on Qwen2-VL-2B), persisted locally, and routed to human expert verification.
    \item \textbf{Epistemic Abstention and Novelty ($|\hat{C}(X_{n+1})| = 0$):} Every candidate taxon exhibits a softmax probability strictly below $1 - \hat{q}_\alpha$. An empty prediction set indicates a residual anomaly that cleared the dual OOD gate but is rejected by all modelled classes -- characteristic of uncatalogued rare species, morphologically distinct instars, or regional invasive taxa. The system executes complete epistemic abstention, initiates an unconstrained nearest-neighbour search across the global exemplar graph, and flags the specimen for immediate priority taxonomic audit.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Active Learning Acquisition via Diverse Gradient Embeddings}
\label{subsec:badge_active_learning}

Observations diverted via epistemic rejection ($\Phi(x) = 0$), set expansion ($|\hat{C}| > k_{\max}$), low top-1 confidence ($\hat{\pi}_{\max} < \tau_{\text{conf}}$), or complete abstention ($|\hat{C}| = 0$) are first written atomically to local persistent storage via \nolinkurl{persist_flagged_observation()} (\nolinkurl{data/flagged_observations/}) to guarantee resilience against object store network partitions. They are subsequently ingested into \nolinkurl{triage_queue.lance} on the in-cluster SeaweedFS object store, capturing the compressed WebP visual crop, the penultimate backbone feature representation $z(x)$, and the unnormalised logit vector $f(x)$. The backlog is synchronised with the DagsHub Label Studio annotation workspace. Because expert taxonomic review is strictly capacity-constrained, processing the backlog in first-in, first-out order is suboptimal: environmental bursts (such as seasonal insect emergences) frequently flood the queue with hundreds of visually redundant specimens. To maximise the marginal information gain per annotation hour, the triage engine orders pending candidates using Batch Active Learning by Diverse Gradient Embeddings (BADGE) \cite{ash2020deep, ren2021deepactive}.

For each unlabelled candidate $x$ in the triage pool $\mathcal{U}$ with penultimate backbone feature representation $z(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d = 384$ for DINOv3-ViT-S/14; $d = 512$ for BioCLIP-2; $d = 768$ for ViT-B/16 baselines), let $\hat{y} = \arg\max_j \hat{\pi}_j(x)$ denote the model's current hallucinated pseudo-label. BADGE constructs a hypothetical gradient embedding $g_x \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times K}$ by evaluating the gradient of the cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$ with respect to the weights $W = (w_1, \dots, w_K)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$ of the final linear classification layer. For each class block $j \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, the $d$-dimensional gradient component $g_x^{(j)}$ admits the closed-form expression:
\begin{equation}
g_x^{(j)} = \frac{\partial}{\partial w_j} \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}\left(f(x), \hat{y}\right) = \left( \hat{\pi}_j(x) - \mathbb{I}(\hat{y} = j) \right) \cdot z(x)
\label{eq:badge_gradient}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ denotes the indicator function. Equation~\ref{eq:badge_gradient} simultaneously encodes both predictive uncertainty and visual representation geometry: when the classifier is uncertain, the scalar residual $(\hat{\pi}_j(x) - \mathbb{I}(\hat{y} = j))$ is large, amplifying the Euclidean norm $\|g_x\|_2$; meanwhile, the outer-product structure with $z(x)$ preserves the directional orientation of the specimen in feature space.

To assemble an annotation batch $\mathcal{B} \subset \mathcal{U}$ of size $B$, the acquisition module executes $k$-means++ seeding over the set of gradient embeddings $\{g_x : x \in \mathcal{U}\}$, iteratively sampling centroids with probability proportional to their squared Euclidean distance from previously selected points. This procedure guarantees that the resulting batch $\mathcal{B}$ spans high-magnitude gradients (high epistemic uncertainty) that are mutually orthogonal in parameter space (high morphological diversity).

Once validated by human taxonomists in Label Studio, confirmed observations are committed back into the DVC-versioned dataset manifests on a scheduled cadence. The newly labelled specimens augment the training split for underrepresented long-tail taxa and refresh the exchangeable calibration holdout $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$. On subsequent CI/CD training cycles, this closed feedback loop refines the linear decision boundaries and tightens the empirical quantile $\hat{q}_\alpha$, progressively reducing average conformal set cardinality $\mathbb{E}[|\hat{C}(X)|]$ whilst preserving the $1 - \alpha$ marginal coverage invariant.
